# Best Scorpion alternatives for trade businesses

[Scorpion](https://www.scorpion.co/) is a big home-services marketing company. Website, SEO, ads, intake tools, and a long list of industries under one roof. For a multi-truck shop with a serious ad budget, that model can work.

For a lot of independent plumbers, HVAC companies, electricians, roofers, and contractors, the fit is different. Contracts run long. Pricing is not published on the site. When you leave, you often get static website files and have to rebuild on a CMS. Those are the reasons owners search for **Scorpion alternatives**.

This guide is for trade owners who want more booked jobs without guessing. It covers when Scorpion still makes sense, what to check before you switch, and practical alternatives - including [Kalvez](/), an AI platform built for crews who need to stay on the tools.

## What Scorpion is (and who it fits)

Scorpion sells a full marketing stack: technology, advertising, and marketing aimed at revenue. Their home-services vertical covers electrical, HVAC, landscaping, pest, plumbing, roofing, and more. They also work with legal, medical, franchise, and multi-location brands.

That breadth is a strength if you want one vendor for a large operation. It can be a mismatch if you are a single-location trade shop that mainly needs:

- A clear Google Business Profile
- A fast website that turns searches into calls
- Local SEO that matches the towns you actually serve
- Honest ownership of your accounts and content when you leave

Scorpion’s own FAQ states typical terms clearly: about **12 months** for marketing technology and SEO, while digital ads (Google Ads, Local Services Ads, Meta, and similar) are often month to month. On exit after the contract, they say they transfer ownership of key accounts such as GA4, Local Services Ads, social accounts, and website content, and they provide static site files and design assets. They also say you will need another vendor to rebuild the site on a CMS you choose.

None of that is hidden. It is still a different commitment than a flexible local partner or an AI platform scoped to the trades.

## Why trade owners look for Scorpion alternatives

Owners rarely leave because “marketing stopped mattering.” They leave when the cost, control, or day-to-day fit stops matching the shop.

Common triggers:

- **Cost stacking.** Management fees plus ad spend plus setup can climb fast for a one-location crew.
- **Contract length.** A year of SEO and technology is a long bet if you are still proving the channel.
- **Website ownership.** Static files on exit mean a rebuild before your next partner can edit the site.
- **Attention model.** Large agencies optimize for scale. Independent shops need plain talk, trade-specific pages, and someone who understands emergency vs install work.
- **Basics first.** Paid ads amplify whatever you already are. If the listing and site leak calls, a bigger agency fee does not fix that.

If any of those sound familiar, you are not alone. The goal is not to trash a big vendor. The goal is to pick the right size of help for your next twelve months of booked jobs.

## How to compare any Scorpion alternative

Before you read brand names, write down five answers. Put them in the proposal from every vendor you talk to.

- **Published or quoted pricing.** What is the monthly fee, what is ad spend, and what is one-time setup?
- **Contract length.** Month to month, six months, or twelve? What happens if you pause ads?
- **Account ownership.** Is the Google Ads account in your name? What about GA4, Local Services Ads, Meta, and the domain?
- **Website exit.** Do you leave with an editable site, or static files that need a rebuild?
- **Local Services Ads.** Who manages LSA, and is that included or a separate line?

Ask for those answers in writing. A good partner will not dodge them. If a sales deck only talks about “growth” and “brand,” keep asking until the ownership and exit terms are clear.

Also decide your stage:

- **Foundation.** Listing incomplete, weak site, few reviews, phone missed after hours. Fix visibility and conversion before heavy ad spend.
- **Growth.** Basics work. You need steady local SEO, better service pages, and careful ads.
- **Scale.** Multiple locations, large budgets, intake teams, and reporting across markets. Full-service agencies fit better here.

Most independent trade shops sit in foundation or early growth. That is where lighter, trade-focused options usually win.

This path costs less in fees and more in your time. It fails when the owner never finishes the site or never replies to reviews. If you will not protect two hours a week for follow-through, buy a done-for-you option instead.

## What to do before you leave a marketing vendor

Switching vendors is where rankings and tracking get messy. Do this before you cancel anything.

- Export Google Analytics / GA4 access and confirm you are an owner, not a guest.
- Confirm Google Business Profile ownership and managers.
- Screenshot Local Services Ads settings and lead history.
- List every domain, hosting login, call-tracking number, and form destination.
- Download website content, blogs, and lead forms.
- Note which phone numbers are tracking numbers vs the real shop line.
- Ask in writing what you receive on exit and when.

If your current site only exists inside a closed platform, budget time for a rebuild. Do not turn off the old site until redirects, NAP consistency (name, address, phone), and the new call path are tested on a phone.

## Red flags in any “Scorpion alternative” pitch

Walk away - or at least slow down - when you hear:

- Guaranteed page-one rankings for competitive city terms
- “We own the ads account for optimization” with no path to transfer
- No written exit plan for the website
- Pressure to sign a year before they audit your listing and intake
- Reports full of impressions and no talk of booked jobs
- One template site reused across unrelated trades

Good partners talk about calls, booked jobs, response time, and the towns you serve. Bad partners sell fear of competitors and a locked box of tools.

## FAQ: Scorpion alternatives

### Is Scorpion bad for contractors?

No. Scorpion can be a fit for larger home-services companies that want a consolidated marketing department and can support the contract and budget. It is often a heavy fit for small independent shops that mainly need local visibility and a converting site.

### What is the biggest risk when leaving Scorpion?

Website transfer. Their public FAQ says you get static files and will need another vendor to rebuild on a CMS. Plan the rebuild and redirects before you cut over. Also confirm ads, LSA, and analytics ownership in writing.

### How much should a trade shop spend on marketing?

Enough to fix the leak first, then enough to buy attention. There is no universal number. A shop missing half its after-hours calls will waste any ad budget. Start with listing, site, and intake. Add spend when the path to a booked job is clear.

### Can I replace a big agency with an AI platform?

If your needs are local SEO, a trade website, and clearer Google presence, yes - that is exactly the gap [Kalvez](/how-it-works) is built to cover. If you need complex multi-location ad ops and a large creative team, keep a full-service agency in the mix.

### Should I pause Google Ads while I switch vendors?

Not blindly. Pause wasteful campaigns. Keep the tracking numbers and landing pages working until the new site and call path are tested. Sudden blackouts confuse customers and reset learning in ad accounts you may still own.
