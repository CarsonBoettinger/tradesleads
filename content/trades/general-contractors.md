## How owners hire a GC online

The journey often looks like this:

1. They search "general contractor near me" or a project term like "home addition contractor."
2. They scan Map Pack results and websites.
3. They shortlist based on reviews, photos, and whether the company looks organized.
4. They call or request a consult.

If your portfolio lives only on a phone, online it looks like you have nothing to show. That is a marketing problem, not a craft problem.

### Searches that drive GC opportunities

- general contractor near me
- home remodeling contractor
- kitchen / bath remodel contractor (if you want those)
- home addition contractor
- custom home builder (only if accurate)
- commercial tenant improvement (if you do commercial)
- insurance restoration contractor (if you do that work)

Be specific about the project types you want. A GC who wants additions should not only rank generically while specialty remodelers take the niche searches.

## Google Business Profile for general contractors

Your profile should feel like a job-site trailer that is organized: clear services, real photos, recent reviews. It feeds the Map Pack. For many owners, it is the first proof you are still active and worth a call.

### Profile essentials

- Correct categories for general contractor / construction
- Services aligned to renovations, additions, builds you sell
- Service area that matches where you bid
- Project photos with short captions
- Weekly or regular posting of finished work when possible
- Fast responses to messages and review replies
- A phone number that rings a human or a clear answering path

Owners hire confidence. An active profile signals you are still in business and proud of the work. Keep name, address, and phone identical on the profile, the website, and major directories. Mixed info slows Google and confuses people who already decided to call you.

## Local SEO for project-based trades

GC local SEO is a mix of Map Pack visibility and a website that proves capability. Relevance, distance, and prominence still apply. Relevance means your listing and pages match how owners describe the job. Distance means you actually bid that area. Prominence means reviews, photos, and consistent business info that show you are established.

Priorities:

1. Google profile with ongoing photo and review activity
2. Project and service pages that match how owners describe work
3. Portfolio that is easy to browse on mobile
4. Clear contact path for consults - click-to-call plus a simple form
5. Consistent NAP and strong reviews

### Site sections that win bids earlier

- Home page with project-type paths
- Renovation / remodel page
- Additions page
- New builds page (if applicable)
- Process page: consult → scope → build → handover
- Project gallery with real jobs
- Reviews / testimonials
- Contact

Explain how you communicate during a project. Owners fear silence more than dust. Marketing that addresses communication wins meetings. Put the phone number high on every key page. Forms help for planned remodel consults. Click-to-call still matters when someone wants a quick conversation after work hours. If you use call tracking, keep the number consistent on the site and GBP so you can see which pages drive real conversations without confusing Google.

## Bidding pages that reduce tire-kickers

A good GC site can qualify leads without being rude. Ask for project type, timeline, and budget range on the consult form - while still offering a phone number. Owners who are serious will answer. Your team saves time. Marketing that attracts the wrong bids is expensive.

Keep the qualification short. Long applications feel like paperwork. A few clear fields plus a visible phone number is enough. Follow up the same day when you can. Slow replies kill web leads faster than a plain site.

## Portfolio, subs, and aftercare

You do not need a directory of every trade partner on the homepage. You do need to show you can coordinate a full project. A simple process diagram and a few partner logos or trade categories is enough. Keep the focus on outcomes for the owner.

If you help owners phase work or navigate financing partners, a short plain-language section helps. Avoid burying important planning info in PDFs nobody opens on a phone. Mobile-first explanations win modern remodel shoppers.

If you stand behind your work with a clear warranty or walkthrough process, put it on the site. Owners fear unfinished punch lists. Marketing that promises a clean handover is not fluff when your operations can deliver it.

## Mistakes that lose GC work online

- Portfolio trapped on Instagram with no site home
- No differentiation between project types
- Outdated website that looks abandoned
- Ignoring Google reviews because "we get referrals"
- Slow follow-up on web leads
- Stock images of houses you never built
- Hiding the phone behind forms only
- Listing every suburb you might bid without proof you work there

Bigger firms look safer because they look organized. Your site and listing are where you close that perception gap. Referrals and search work together. When a past client says your name, the owner will Google you. What they find should confirm the praise in under a minute: reviews, photos, phone number, and a clear sense you handle projects like theirs.

## Common questions

### Do GCs need town pages?

Sometimes. If you truly dominate a few towns and have distinct proof there, useful local pages can help. Thin duplicates across every suburb rarely help.

### How many project photos are enough?

More than you think, updated often. A gallery that stopped in 2021 feels like a company that slowed down. Add new jobs as you finish them.

### Should I list every sub-trade I coordinate?

List the project outcomes you sell. Owners search for the result (addition, kitchen, whole-home remodel) more than your internal trade stack.

### What is the fastest trust fix?

Recent Google reviews plus a mobile gallery of real projects and an obvious way to request a consult. Then deepen service pages.
