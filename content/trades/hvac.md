## Google Business Profile for HVAC companies

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is your front door on Google. It shows on maps and in local results with hours, services, photos, and reviews. An incomplete profile costs you peak-season calls.

Claim and verify the listing. Use your real business name. Set the primary category to HVAC contractor or the closest accurate match. Add secondary categories for heating or air conditioning only when you truly offer those services. Keep your service area accurate. Listing towns you never cover hurts trust and performance.

Fill out services in GBP. List maintenance, repair, and installation for heating and cooling. Add indoor air quality or duct work if that is part of your business. Post updates during busy seasons. A short note about tune-ups or emergency response keeps the profile fresh.

Use real photos: units you installed, technicians on site, branded vans. Avoid glossy stock. Update hours for holiday on-call schedules. Keep the same phone number on GBP, your website, and major directories. Consistency helps Google and customers.

## Getting into the Map Pack

The Map Pack is the map plus three local businesses at the top of many searches. For "AC repair near me" and similar queries, those three spots take most of the calls. Getting there takes relevance, distance, and prominence.

Relevance means your profile and website match the search. Distance means you serve that area. Prominence means proof you are established: reviews, accurate citations, links, and real customer engagement.

There is no single switch for Map Pack rank. Complete GBP. Publish service pages that match how people search. Earn reviews on a steady cadence. Keep name, address, and phone identical everywhere. When Google sees you as the clear local HVAC option, you move up.

Focus on the jobs that pay: emergency AC repair, furnace repair, heat pump service, system replacement, and maintenance plans. Build categories and site pages around those offers instead of chasing every vague phrase.

## Service pages that match seasonal demand

Your homepage cannot carry every search. People look for specific HVAC problems and equipment. Give them pages that answer those searches.

Create dedicated pages for AC repair, AC installation, furnace repair, furnace replacement, heat pumps, ductless systems, and maintenance plans if you offer them. Each page should explain the service, who it is for, what the visit looks like, and how to call. Write like you talk to a homeowner. Skip buzzwords.

Location pages help if you cover several cities. Mention real conditions: older homes with aging furnaces, new builds needing proper sizing, humidity in summer. Do not spin the same paragraph across twenty towns. Unique, honest detail wins.

Put the phone number high on the page. On mobile, the call action should be impossible to miss. HVAC searches spike when comfort fails. Remove friction. Do not put a long form before the number.

## Reviews that win high-intent calls

Reviews decide trust when the house is hot or freezing. Volume, recency, and detail all matter. A company with dozens of recent reviews that mention AC repair or furnace installs will beat a competitor with a few old ratings.

Ask after every good job. Send a text with a direct review link. Coach techs to mention it when the customer is happy. Respond to reviews. Thank people for specifics. Address problems calmly when something went wrong. Ignoring a negative review looks careless.

Ask customers to mention the equipment or service when it feels natural. Do not buy reviews. Earn them through good work and a simple ask. Protect reputation by keeping response times tight during peak season.

## Call CTAs built for urgency

HVAC websites often bury the phone number under banners and blog links. That design fails when someone needs service today. Lead with the call.

Use a sticky call button on mobile. Keep the number in the header on desktop. For emergency repair pages, say "Call now." For replacement and maintenance pages, "Get a quote" works, but still show the phone. Many homeowners want to talk through options. Use real click-to-call links. If you use call tracking, keep one consistent number on the site and Google profile so you can see which pages drive conversations without confusing Google or callers.

Place CTAs after proof: star ratings, a short process section, brands you service. Avoid pop-ups that cover the call link on a phone. Page speed counts. Heavy carousels slow the first tap. Compress media. Keep the layout clean. The site should answer "Can these people help me today?" and then connect the call.

## Common HVAC marketing mistakes

Owners often overspend on ads while the GBP sits half empty. Others launch a pretty site with no repair or install pages. Some stuff every city in the county into the footer and wonder why leads are junk. Inconsistent NAP across directories creates the same confusion for Google.

Another mistake is writing for algorithms instead of homeowners. Walls of keyword repeats do not convert. People want to know if you service their brand, how fast you can arrive, and whether you handle repair and replacement.

Ignoring seasonality is costly. Update offers before summer and winter peaks. Promote maintenance when the rush slows. Explain how estimates work. Treating local SEO as a one-week project fails. Profiles, reviews, and pages need ongoing care.

### FAQ

#### Is Google Business Profile enough for an HVAC company?

It is essential, but not enough on its own. A clear website supports rankings, explains services, and converts people who click through from search. Use both together.

#### Which HVAC pages should I publish first?

Start with AC repair, furnace repair, and system installation or replacement. Add maintenance plans and specialty pages like heat pumps once the core money pages are live.

#### How many reviews do I need to compete?

More than your top local competitors is a useful target, but recency and detail matter as much as totals. Aim for a steady stream every week rather than a one-time blitz.

#### How long does HVAC local SEO take?

Profile fixes and review momentum can help within weeks. Stronger Map Pack movement and service-page gains often take a few months, depending on competition and how complete your presence is now.
