## How roofing jobs start online

Storm season is a race. After bad weather, everyone searches at once. The crews that appear first in the Map Pack get the inspections. Even in quiet months, people research before they let anyone on the roof.

Typical search paths:

- roofer / roofing contractor near me
- roof repair [town]
- roof leak repair
- storm damage roof inspection
- roof replacement cost (then they call the companies that look solid)
- flat roof repair
- emergency tarp / temporary roof repair

Your marketing should cover repair and replacement clearly. Many sites only shout "new roofs" and miss the repair searches that fill the calendar between big jobs. Referrals still matter. They do not replace search. Even a neighbor tip usually ends with "let me look them up first."

## Google Business Profile for roofers

The Map Pack is where storm traffic lands. For many roofers, the Google Business Profile drives more first calls than the website.

### Listing checklist

- Correct primary category (Roofing contractor)
- Services: repair, replacement, inspections, materials you actually install
- Service area that matches crew capacity - not the whole state
- Photos: finished roofs, before/after storm work, crew on site, gutters if relevant
- Products/services descriptions in plain language
- Accurate hours, including storm-response windows if you take urgent calls
- A phone number that rings a human or a clear answering path
- Regular posts during storm season with useful updates - not spam

Keep the business name clean. Keyword stuffing in the title looks desperate and can create compliance headaches. Google notices. Customers notice too.

### Reviews close the gap with bigger brands

Homeowners compare you to national names. Reviews and local photos are how a local crew feels safe. Ask after every completed job while the customer is still glad you came. Make the link easy: a short text beats a paper card that gets lost. Reply to reviews - especially after storms, when emotions run high. Thank the good ones. Address the hard ones without arguing in public.

A 4.8 with recent reviews beats a 5.0 with three reviews from years ago. Review velocity matters as much as the average.

## Local SEO that wins inspections

Roofing local SEO is practical:

- Strong Google profile with ongoing reviews
- Service pages for repair, replacement, storm damage, and inspections
- Fast mobile site with click-to-call
- Clear proof: photos, process, insurance/workmanship signals
- Consistent NAP (name, address, phone) across the web
- Honest town mentions on pages you can actually serve

Storm pages should be honest. Do not promise free inspections everywhere if you cannot staff them. Say what you can do and how to request a visit.

### Website pages that convert roofing leads

Useful structure:

1. Home with paths for Repair / Replace / Storm damage
2. Roof repair page
3. Roof replacement page
4. Storm damage / insurance claim support page (careful, accurate language)
5. Materials or roof types you install
6. Gallery of real jobs
7. Contact with phone-first CTA

Each page should say what you do, where you do it, why you are a safe call, and how to reach you. Quotes die without proof. Show the work. Explain the inspection steps. Put the phone number where a thumb can hit it.

## Call CTAs that convert on mobile

Most roof searches happen on a phone. Keep a sticky call button. Repeat the number near the top of every service page. Use real click-to-call links. Forms help for planned replacements. They fail for water coming through the ceiling at night. Match the CTA to the job: call for urgent work, form or call for estimates. If you use call tracking, keep one consistent number on the site and Google profile so you can see which pages drive conversations without confusing Google or callers.

## Mistakes that bury local roofers

- Letting lead mills and nationals own the Map Pack unchallenged
- A site with no recent photos
- Hiding the phone number behind forms only
- One thin page for every roofing need
- Ignoring reviews until a bad storm hits
- Slow mobile experience when people are standing under a leak
- Outdated hours during storm season

Also watch your GBP messaging during storms. If hours or temporary response times change, update them. Customers hate calling a listing that says open when nobody answers. Fix the basics before buying ads. Ads amplify whatever you already are.

## Between storms: stay visible

Quiet months are when you fix the foundation:

- Refresh photos from recent jobs
- Tighten service pages
- Catch up on review requests
- Confirm NAP consistency
- Prepare storm landing content before the season
- Staff the phone path you advertise

When the weather turns, you want to be ready - not starting from a neglected profile. Storm weeks punish thin listings. Quiet months are when that prep pays.

## Insurance and storm language that stays honest

Homeowners often ask about insurance claims after storms. Your marketing can explain that you document damage and support the process without promising outcomes you do not control. Clear, calm language builds trust. Hype creates disputes later. Say what you do: inspect, document, scope, and communicate. Train whoever answers the phone on the same script.

## Common questions

### Should roofers buy leads?

Some do. Many regret low-quality leads. Strengthening your own Map Pack and site often costs less per booked job over time. If you buy leads, send them to a page that matches the promise and answers fast.

### Do before-and-after photos help SEO?

They help customers first. That improves engagement and trust. Fresh, real job photos on your profile and site support local visibility more than generic stock images.

### How fast can storm SEO help?

Listing and review improvements can help relatively quickly. Winning competitive Map Pack spots in busy markets takes consistent work before peak season. Think weeks and months, not overnight miracles.

### What is the first fix after a bad storm season?

Usually the phone path and review response. Then photos and repair/replacement page clarity. Then deeper ranking work.
