## How landscaping customers search

Outdoor work is seasonal and visual. People search with the task and the season in mind:

- landscaper near me
- yard cleanup
- landscape design and install
- mulch / bed maintenance
- lawn care (if you offer it)
- hardscape / patio / retaining wall (if you build it)
- tree / shrub planting
- drainage / grading help (if relevant)

Be clear about design-build versus maintenance. Mixing both without clarity confuses customers and dilutes pages.

### Photos are not optional

Landscaping sells through pictures. Before-and-after cleanups, night lighting, patios, and healthy beds do more than paragraphs. Put fresh photos on Google and on the site. Rotate them as the season moves. Stock images of perfect yards nobody recognizes look fake. Real jobs from your routes win trust.

If you build patios and also plant beds, separate the proof. Hardscape shoppers want structural photos and drainage notes. Softscape shoppers want plant health and design sense. Mixing everything into one blurry gallery makes both audiences unsure.

## Google Business Profile for landscapers

Your profile should look busy in a good way: recent photos, recent reviews, clear services. It feeds the Map Pack. For spring cleanups and installs, those map results often decide who gets the first call of the season.

### Listing priorities

- Category fit (Landscaper / Landscape designer / Lawn care as accurate)
- Services that match packages you sell
- Service area neighborhoods and towns you can route efficiently
- Seasonal hours updates
- Photos every few weeks during peak season
- Posts for spring cleanup, fall cleanup, and install openings when true
- A phone number that rings a human or a clear answering path

Ask for reviews after cleanups and installs. Neighborhood proof wins the street. A review that mentions a subdivision or town helps the next nearby homeowner feel safe. Keep name, address, and phone consistent across the profile, website, and directories. Mixed info hurts Map Pack performance.

In many towns, HOA rules shape what you can install. A short note that you work within HOA guidelines - when true - reduces fear. It is practical marketing for suburban routes.

## Local SEO for outdoor services

Local SEO for landscapers focuses on route density and seasonal intent. Relevance means your pages match cleanup, install, or maintenance searches. Distance means you can actually get to those yards. Prominence means reviews, photos, and a profile that looks alive.

Do this well:

1. Keep Google active with photos and reviews
2. Build pages for cleanup, maintenance, and install/hardscape lines you sell
3. Make the site mobile-fast with a clear call or quote CTA
4. Show service areas honestly
5. Collect reviews that mention nearby streets or towns when natural

### Website structure that books the season

- Home with Cleanup / Maintenance / Design & install paths
- Spring / fall cleanup pages
- Maintenance packages page
- Hardscape / patio page if offered
- Drainage / problem-yard page if you solve those jobs
- Gallery
- Contact / quote request with phone option

Spell out what is included in a cleanup. Ambiguity creates price shopping and no-shows. Clarity creates booked routes. Put click-to-call near the top on mobile. Forms help for installs and design consults. Same-day cleanup seekers often just want the number. If you use call tracking, keep the tracked number consistent on key pages so you can see which offers drive real conversations.

Many of the best landscaping jobs start as problem yards: mud, standing water, dead slopes. If you solve drainage or grading issues, create a page for it. Those searches are often less crowded than generic landscaping and attract serious homeowners.

## Turning one-off cleanups into routes

Marketing does not end when the cleanup is done. Same-week review request. Same-week offer for monthly maintenance if you sell it. A simple follow-up path turns a frantic spring customer into a summer route stop. Your site should make that maintenance offer obvious.

Many landscaping estimates die because nobody follows up. Build a simple habit: same-day thank you, two-day check-in, and a photo of a similar finished yard when helpful. Your website should make reopening the conversation easy with a visible phone number and a quote form that actually gets answered.

Use slower months to rebuild the gallery, request missing reviews, and pre-book spring cleanups with past clients. Digital follow-up is part of landscaping marketing now. Crews that only hustle in April leave money in February.

## Mistakes that leave landscapers invisible

- No Google photos all winter, then panic in March
- One page for every outdoor service
- Ignoring reviews because crews are "too busy in season"
- Website that only shows a Facebook link
- Service area so wide the profile looks unserious
- No follow-up after estimates
- Hiding the phone behind long forms
- Advertising hard in spring while the listing and site stay thin

Spring is won in late winter. Update the profile and site before the phones should ring. Paid ads can help at open, but organic listing strength compounds across seasons. Fix Google and the site first so every channel works better.

## Common questions

Yes if you sell both - use clear navigation so customers self-select. If lawn care is a separate brand, keep listings honest and avoid confusing duplicate profiles.

### Do seasonal posts help?
