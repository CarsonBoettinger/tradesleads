## Google Business Profile is your storefront

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is often the first thing people see. It sits next to the map. It shows hours, phone number, photos, and reviews. If that profile is thin or wrong, you lose jobs before your website loads.

Claim the profile if you have not already. Use the exact business name customers know. Set the primary category to plumber. Add secondary categories only when they match real work you do, like water heater installation or drain cleaning. Keep your service area honest. If you do not cover a town, do not list it.

Post updates when you can. A short note about emergency service or a completed job keeps the profile active. Upload real job photos: vans, pipe work, crews on site. Stock images look fake. Homeowners notice.

Hours matter. If you offer after-hours emergency service, say so. Wrong hours create angry callers and bad reviews. Keep the phone number the same across your site, GBP, and directories. Mixed numbers confuse Google and customers.

## Winning the Map Pack

The Map Pack is the three businesses Google shows with the map at the top of local results. For emergency and same-day plumbing, that pack often decides who gets the call. Rankings lean on relevance, distance, and prominence.

Relevance means your profile and site match what people search. Distance means you serve that area. Prominence means signals that you are a real business: reviews, citations, links, and consistent info.

You cannot buy your way in with one trick. Complete your GBP. Earn reviews. Publish clear service pages for the towns you serve. Keep NAP (name, address, phone) consistent. When Google trusts that you are the local plumber for a search, you rise.

Track which searches bring calls. Burst pipe. Clogged drain. Water heater repair. Focus content and categories on the jobs you want most. Chasing every keyword dilutes the message.

## Service pages that match how people search

A homepage alone is not enough. Homeowners search for specific problems. Your site should answer those searches with dedicated pages.

Create pages for core services: drain cleaning, leak detection, water heater repair and replacement, sewer line work, fixture installs, emergency plumbing. Each page should say what you do, where you do it, what to expect, and how to call. Use plain language. Skip filler about "solutions" and "excellence."

Local pages help when you serve multiple towns. Mention real neighborhoods and response expectations. Do not copy-paste the same block into twenty cities. Write what is true for that area.

Put your phone number near the top of every service page. A short form is fine, but the call button should be obvious on mobile. Most plumbing leads are urgent. Make the next step one tap.

## Reviews that build trust fast

Reviews are proof. When someone has water on the floor, they pick the company that looks reliable. Recent reviews with real detail beat a few old five-star blurbs.

Ask after every completed job. Text a link. Train the crew to mention it when the customer is happy. Respond to every review. Thank people. Fix problems in public when you can. Silence on a bad review looks worse than a calm reply.

Encourage specifics. "Fixed our water heater same day and cleaned up" helps more than "Great job!" Specific reviews also support the services you want to rank for. Never buy reviews or trade discounts for stars. Earn them the slow way. It lasts.

## Call CTAs that convert under stress

Plumbing sites fail when the call to action is buried. Big headline. Pretty photo. Tiny phone number in the footer. That layout loses emergency work.

On mobile, keep a sticky call button or a clear header phone link. Use "Call now" for urgent services. For planned work, "Request a quote" is fine, but still show the number. Real click-to-call links matter more than a number people have to copy. If you use call tracking, keep one consistent number on the site and Google profile so you can see which pages drive conversations without confusing Google or callers.

Repeat the CTA after proof points: reviews, service lists, and a short "what happens next" section. Avoid pop-ups that block the number on a small screen. If the site loads slowly on a phone, people bounce back to the Map Pack and call someone else. Compress images. Keep the layout simple. Your site's job is to get the call.

## Common mistakes that cost plumbers jobs

Many companies spend money in the wrong places. Paid ads without a solid GBP. A site rebuilt every year with no service pages. Keyword stuffing. Fake service areas. Outdated photos of a truck you sold years ago.

Another mistake is hiding process details. You do not need a public price list for every job. Explain how estimates work and what the first visit looks like. Clarity reduces no-shows.

Ignoring mobile is a quiet killer. Most searches happen on phones next to a leak. Tiny buttons and long forms lose work. Treating SEO as a one-time project also fails. Profiles, reviews, and pages need ongoing attention when you add services or towns.

### FAQ

#### Do I need a website if my Google Business Profile is strong?

Yes. GBP wins many Map Pack clicks, but people still check your site for services, proof, and confidence. A clear site also supports rankings and converts visitors who find you beyond the map.

#### How often should plumbers ask for reviews?

Ask after every satisfied job. A short text with a direct link works well. Steady weekly reviews beat a big push once a year.

#### What service pages should I create first?

Start with your highest-margin and most frequent jobs: drains, leaks, water heaters, and emergency service. Add specialty pages next once the core pages are solid.

#### How long until local SEO shows results?

Some GBP fixes help within weeks. Stronger Map Pack and service-page gains often take a few months of consistent work. Speed depends on competition in your towns and how complete your presence is today.
